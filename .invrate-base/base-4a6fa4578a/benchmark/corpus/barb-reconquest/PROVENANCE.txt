Source: live Europe game (human sweettea + 6 deep + 6 classic/def +
civil-war splinters), autosave freeciv-T0160-Y01090-auto.sav.zst,
taken 2026-07-31. Analysis: doc/deep-ai/findings/civ2civ3-adjustments.md,
"T160 autosave forensics".

The world it preserves: under HORDES, the Pirate (player 13) and
Barbarian (player 14) players hold 52 captured cities, 47 of them
with NO garrison, for ~40 turns -- while every nation's military,
deep and classic alike, is defensive infantry pinned at
AIUNIT_DEFEND_HOME. A quarter of the map is free for the walking and
nobody walks. Player ids 13/14 are hardcoded in assert.sh as position
facts.

Prep applied to the raw autosave: the human seat (player 12, Irish,
"Aoife MacMurrough") was aitoggled to a classic AI and the game
resaved, so the position runs headless with the world as-lived --
deliberately NOT converted to a deep seat, which would change the
story under test.

What it exercises: singleton operations (required==1 formation),
garrison drafting (sparable DEFEND_HOME blades join a formation the
free pool cannot fill, survival-gated), barbarian non-reinforcement
pricing (barb_reinforce_rate), snap-up attacker builds, and the
defence-first auction under real horde pressure. (An adjacent
walk-in sortie was prototyped and dropped: citymindist makes
city-adjacent-to-city geometry impossible, so the sortie could
never fire -- the singleton op owns all distances instead.)

Expected before the T160 fix stack: mechanism asserts FAIL
(singleton targets are declined by formation). Expected after: all
asserts pass within TURNS=12; the 12-turn proof run retook 9 of the
52 barb-held tiles where the live game retook 0 in ~40 turns.
