## 3. Why the first gov-centre formulation was wrong

The credit compared "the nearest centre today" with "the nearest
centre if this city becomes one" — and left the existing centre
standing. The Palace and the Ecclesiastical Palace are **one per
player**: building either here destroys the one over there.

So every city in the empire became a permanent Palace candidate. The
counters say it in absolute numbers:

    weight 1.0, isolated-150, 20 games      firings/game   mean credit
    gov centre, BEFORE the move fix              3454          2827
    gov centre, AFTER  the move fix               357          2350
    the whole existing horizon ladder, OFF arm    ---            85

**A credit 33x the size of the entire ladder it competes in, fired ten
times as often as it should have been.** Score -49.40, cities -2.83,
wonders -1.32, 0/20 seeds.

The fix makes the term the difference between two whole states —
today's centres, and the centres that survive the move plus this
candidate — and charges the city that LOSES the improvement the waste
it starts paying. A relocation that helps nobody is now worth nothing
without that having to be asserted anywhere in the code.

**Method note this earns:** the audit's ranking was right about the
effect and silent about the mechanic. Pricing an effect requires
knowing whether the building that carries it is additive or unique,
and nothing in the effects tables says which.

## 4. Culture: the arithmetic is right and the venue is wrong

At weight 1.0 the culture credit is not oversized the way the gov
centre was. Its mean is 131 want against the ladder's 85 — the same
order. What it does is **change how many things get credit at all**:

    isolated-150, 20 games        credited improvements   mean credit
    OFF                                    414 586            85.1
    CULT (culture = 1.0)                   830 533           144.1
      of which the culture term             685 394           131.0

Culture doubles the credited population, because on civ2civ3 thirty-
eight effects carry History and a Temple is buildable in every city
from the start of the game. The cell paid for it in exactly the column
the derby said deep was already short of: **cities -1.18, settlers
-0.91, pop -12.46, techs -1.41, score -19.43 (t -5.55).**

This is not a refutation of the derby's finding. It is the derby's own
caveat, measured: culture is **14.5% of world score at T150** and
29.5% at T300. At the tuning cells' horizon, paying culture at the
score's exchange rate buys 14.5% of the metric with shields that were
buying the other 85.5%.

Two things follow, and both are for the next session:

- **The weight is the wrong lever to test first; the venue is.** The
  credit already scales with the turns that remain, so the question is
  whether a T300+ cell pays for it. That is garden2b, primary (T300)
  or largepox-200 — and section 2 says why none of them ran.
- **The `war=` exemption is worth its own arm.** 0.65 of the culture
  credits landed in cities the survival model calls threatened. The
  derby's diagnosis was that deep's wartime gate held zero Temples for
  six hundred turns; whether lifting that gate is what costs the
  expansion column, or whether it is the credit itself, is one A/B
  and nobody has run it.

**Correction to the evidence base (arithmetic, not opinion):**
`history_interest_pml = 0` in BOTH `data/civ2civ3/game.ruleset:383`
and `data/classic/game.ruleset:378`. `city_history_gain()` does carry
an interest term and it is **multiplied by zero in every cell this
project runs**. The derby hindsight's "history stock earns interest …
compounds" is true of the formula and inert in the venue; culture
accumulates **linearly**. The credit implements the compounding form
for rulesets that set the field and takes the linear branch here.

**Second correction:** the DARKFX brief says "the derby was classic".
It was not — `europe-derby-hindsight.md` §0(a) records the civ2civ3
ruleset setting `mapsize=PLAYER` on that map. This matters: **classic
has zero History effects**, so had the derby been classic its Temple
and History readings could not have existed.

## 5. Buy on gold-per-shield: the mechanism barely moves

    shields bought per gold spent     OFF      ON     change
    isolated-150                     0.3881  0.3994   +2.9%
    contested                        0.4088  0.4087    0.0%

    emergency-tier share of all buys  31.6% / 33.2%   untouched

The audit's "top-ups are ~4x cheaper per shield" is a fact about the
cost curve and not a fact about the buy list. A third of all purchases
are in the triage tier this change deliberately does not touch, and
within the remaining two-thirds the want spread still dominates the
price spread.

So: **score +5.58 (t +1.64, 13/20) on isolated-150 and -0.12 on
contested, on a mechanism whose own efficiency counter moved 2.9% and
0.0%.** Read the execution counter before the score — the score move
is not attributable to the mechanism that was supposed to cause it,
and the honest reading is one positive direction on one cell with a
mechanism that is not doing much.

## 6. Two items closed on evidence, no code

### Disasters (brief item 6)

The brief's own instruction: if the expected values are negligible,
report the numbers and skip the code. They are.

`check_disasters()` (`server/cityturn.c:4607`) rolls
`game.info.disasters * frequency` against `DISASTER_BASE_RARITY`
= 1 000 000. civ2civ3 sets `disasters = 100` and **all six disasters
have `frequency = 10`**, so every one of them is

    p = 100 x 10 / 1 000 000 = 0.001 per city per turn

against **illness at 10-30 per mille per turn** (`illness_base_factor
= 10`, firing in 20 of 58 cities in the T251 save) — the risk the
plague credit already prices, one to two orders of magnitude larger.
Mfg./Nuclear Plant's `ReducePopulation` expectation over a MORT
horizon is 24 x 0.001 = **0.024 citizens**, about 0.4 want at the
~17-want citizen price this ladder pays. That is below the resolution
of every credit in the file and far below the noise of any cell.

Earthquake's `EmptyProdStock` on a Hills city is the one with a
plausible interaction (it is a shield-stock wipe and deep's site
scorer likes hills), and at 0.1% a turn against a stock that is
rebuilt every few turns it is worth a fraction of a shield per turn.
**Not built. The numbers are the finding.**

### Border push (brief item 8)

**Closed: there is nothing dark to price.** Neither ruleset carries a
single building- or wonder-gated border effect. The only
border-family effect type present in either `effects.ruleset` is
`Tile_Claimable` — four instances in each, terrain-gated, with no
improvement requirement anywhere in their reqs. civ2civ3's border
radius comes from city size and server settings, not from effects, so
there is no valuation venue and no building whose want is missing one.
`site_claim_bonus` remains deep's only border credit and stays where
it is.

## 7. Deferred, with designs

Both were fenced out by the brief's own priority rule once the
structural pair, the buy change and the culture credit had landed.

### Item 4 — specialist type as a decision

**What is true today.** `cm.c:1029-1051` searches per specialist type,
but every AI pins `factor[O_GOLD] == factor[O_SCIENCE] == 2`, so a
taxman and a scientist are indistinguishable to the CM and the choice
falls out of lattice order. `allow_specialists` is read by nothing
(upstream candidate, unchanged).

**The design.** Two PLACEHOLDER knobs, `cm_specialist_gold_gain` and
`cm_specialist_sci_gain`, multiplying the two factors deep already
sets in `deepcm.c`. The state they read exists and is already read
elsewhere in deep: the treasury guard's solvency signal (gold below
the guard means a taxman is worth more than a scientist by exactly the
ratio the guard is short) and the tech-upkeep bill (which is a gold
cost that scales with techs held, so a research-heavy empire's
marginal scientist is partly self-financing). Both default to 1.0 =
off = today's pinned 2/2.

**Why it is small and why it is still worth doing separately.** The
diff is two multiplications; the risk is that the CM is the single
hottest path in the profile and any change there is measured in
`assess_danger`-sized units. Session #28 owns the CM state machine and
this must not drift into it.

### Item 5 — MAX_RATES in government choice

**What is true today.** `EFT_MAX_RATES` returns 0 from
`dai_effect_value`, and both `daidata.c:547-548` and
`advdata.c:1031-1032` carry in-source admissions that re-optimising
rates per candidate government is too expensive. `H_RATES` is set at
every difficulty. So the AI is bound by a ceiling it cannot price:
Despotism 60 to Democracy 90 is invisible to the government choice.

**The design.** A closed-form ceiling term in deep's `gov_value`
wrapper (the hook is already forwarded), priced by R1's marginal-value
machinery rather than by a second search. At the current split, one of
the three rates is at the cap; R1 already computes what a marginal
point of science and of luxury is worth. The value of a higher cap is
therefore the marginal value at the binding constraint times the
number of points the new ceiling unlocks, amortized like everything
else. One PLACEHOLDER weight, `gov_rate_ceiling_gain`, 0 = off.

**The trap to avoid.** The term must read the BINDING constraint and
not the nominal ceiling: an empire that is not at its cap gains
nothing from a higher one, and pricing the ceiling unconditionally
would make Democracy attractive to a player who cannot use it. This is
the "cheapest seam in the tree" from the seam map, and it is cheap
precisely because it does not re-run the optimiser.

## 8. What the next session must know

- **garden2b, primary and any T300 cell are currently unreachable
  from this harness.** Foreground blocks cap at ten minutes, a single
  T300 game exceeds that, and the DONE cache is per-game so a killed
  block loses all in-flight work. Every long-horizon question in this
  project — culture above all — is blocked on this and not on the
  code. Either the cells get a background runner that survives, or the
  long-horizon claims stay unmeasured.
- **The gov-centre and free-upkeep credits are still ~25x the ladder's
  mean at weight 1.0** (2350 and 2734 against 85). The move fix cut
  the FIRING RATE, not the size. Parity weights from this session's
  census would be roughly 0.036 and 0.031, and the arm that would test
  them has not been run. They are the obvious first entries in the
  tuning pile.
- **The structural pair is venue-split and neither side resolves:**
  +2.15 (t +0.83) on isolated-150, -1.89 (t -0.88) on contested. A
  verdict inherits its venue, for the seventh time.
- **All three new credits are civ2civ3-only.** classic has no
  Ecclesiastical Palace, no History effect of any kind, and no
  King Richard's/Statue of Zeus free upkeep, so they are structurally
  inert on every `*-classic` cell. That is a property to state, not a
  defect to fix.
- **The MinSize free-upkeep ladder is not what the brief thought.**
  civ2civ3's sizes-5-to-20 `Unit_Upkeep_Free_Per_City` steps are
  **City**-range Food upkeep, not Player range: they are a growth-side
  term belonging with the capacity credit, not an empire-scope one,
  and this session's credit correctly does not cover them.
