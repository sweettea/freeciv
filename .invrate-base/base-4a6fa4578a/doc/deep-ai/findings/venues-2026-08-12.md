# Two new combat venues, and the counter that could never fire (2026-08-12)

Tasks #51 (air/spy cell) and #57 (naval cell). Branch
`epic/venues-air-naval`, cut from `9ccbf8426e`.

**One line: the air/spy venue works — `tech80air-200` is the first cell
in the portfolio where Flight, Advanced Flight, Espionage and Rocketry
are all live and where air units are built and die. The naval venue
works too, but its stated acceptance bar could not be met by ANY venue:
`deep-wave by_sea` is behind `utype_move_type(ut) == UMT_SEA`, and
`utype_move_type()` returns `UMT_LAND` for every unit class on the
server, because `set_unit_move_type()` is only ever called by the
client.**

---

## 0. The blocking discovery: `utype_move_type()` is dead server-side

Task #57's bar was "deep-wave `by_sea` nonzero". The counter read 0 in
437 440 evaluations on `tech50war-200`, and the standing reading was
that this was `tech50war-200`'s landlocked geometry. It is not.

The reachability arm in `deepcombat.c:590` is

    && !(utype_move_type(at) == UMT_SEA && coastal)

and the census that follows attributes anything that got in by that arm
to `by_sea`. Both are unreachable, because:

    $ grep -rn "set_unit_move_type" --include=*.c .
    common/unittype.c:2961:void set_unit_move_type(struct unit_class *puclass)
    client/packhand.c:3688:    set_unit_move_type(pclass);

`unit_class::move_type` is assigned in exactly one place, and that place
is the **client**, on receipt of the ruleset packet. The server never
calls it, so the field keeps its zero-initialised value, which is
`UMT_LAND` (`unittype.h:133`, `SPECENUM_VALUE0`).

Measured, not inferred. A temporary one-shot dump in the survival wave
(reverted before commit), run on a resumed T140 save of the naval probe:

    TMPCLASS class="Missile"    move_type=0
    TMPCLASS class="Land"       move_type=0
    TMPCLASS class="Small Land" move_type=0
    TMPCLASS class="Big Land"   move_type=0
    TMPCLASS class="Merchant"   move_type=0
    TMPCLASS class="Sea"        move_type=0
    TMPCLASS class="Trireme"    move_type=0
    TMPCLASS class="Helicopter" move_type=0
    TMPCLASS class="Air"        move_type=0

Every class. `UMT_SEA` is 1, so **no server-side `== UMT_SEA` test can
ever be true and no `!= UMT_LAND` test can ever be false.**

The venue was ruled out first, so the code claim rests on positive
evidence rather than on absence. In `nav-n1`'s final save: 94% of
cities (50/53) are coastal, 64 armed Destroyers are alive, 36 of them
on ocean tiles, the nearest enemy warship to every one of those 50
coastal cities is between 0 and 7 tiles away, and 24 player pairs
are at war (of 36 orderings among the 8 seats plus the barbarian
player). A Destroyer moves 6 tiles/turn against a horizon of 3, so
the distance test admits it with 17 tiles to spare. The geometry is as
favourable as it can be made, and `by_sea` was still 0 in 13 294
evaluations.

### What else this touches

`deepdefence.c:1248` already records half of this — that
`utype_move_type()` "reports the TRIREME class as UMT_LAND in civ2civ3"
— and the fix built there, `deep_uclass_swims()` / `deep_uclass_walks()`
asking terrain nativity directly, is the right one. The diagnosis
recorded with it is too narrow: it is not the Trireme class and it is
not civ2civ3. It is every class, in every ruleset, in every server-side
caller, and the remaining call sites were left on the broken accessor:

## 1. Method

Eight calibration games — six configs, and the two winners on a second
seed. `--arms deep` throughout (all eight seats deep, so every seat
produces census lines), jobs 2, ports 7550-7563, worktree binary built
`-Daimodules=classic,tex,baseline,deep`, `KEEP_SAVES=20` so unit
composition can be read as a time series rather than an endpoint. Total
cost 4 262 s of game time, ~36 min of wall clock at jobs 2.

Instruments: the `deep-*` census in `game.log`; the archived savegames
via `benchmark/savequery.py`; the per-research tech bitstring the way
`benchmark/era-check.py` reads it; and, for coastality and overseas
conquest, a flood fill of the final save's terrain rows.

One diagnostic tool is worth keeping in mind: **a finished game can be
resumed for three turns** (`load SAVE` / `set endturn N+3` / `start`)
and it re-emits the full census. Section 0's whole argument was settled
that way in about a minute per iteration, against a T140 autosave,
rather than by replaying 150-turn games.

## 2. AIR/SPY cell (task #51)

### The lever that worked, and the one that did not

`tech50war-200` cannot reach Flight because research is near-frozen
(50 -> ~56 techs in 200 turns), and M4a showed **no cell below T300
reaches Flight at all**. Raising research rate cannot fix that in
affordable wall-clock: Flight sits at 49 recursive prerequisites in
civ2civ3's 87-advance tree, i.e. a player needs ~50 techs before Flight
is even researchable. So the horizon is not the lever — **the start
is**. `techlevel 80` hands out 80 of 87 advances in valid prerequisite
order (`give_initial_techs()` -> `pick_random_tech()`), which puts the
whole W32/W35 toolkit on the board at turn 1.

### Three candidates; the winner on two seeds

Three things fell out, in order of how much they change the design:

- **The horizon decides whether air units ever FIGHT.** At T120 the
  helicopter count only rises, so nothing has been shot down. Attrition
  first appears past ~T140 in every variant. T120 is not a combat
  horizon for this era, whatever the tech table says.
- **`techlossforgiveness` decides whether the ERA SURVIVES the
  horizon.** civ2civ3 sets it to 0; the *server* default is -1, "never
  lose a tech". At 0 a seat in research debt forgets advances, so the
  cell leaks out of the era it was built for: Espionage 6/8 at T120 ->
  4/8 at T200, Combined Arms 5/8 -> 2/8, Rocketry 7/8 -> 5/8. At -1 the
  toolkit not only holds but grows (80 -> 80-83 techs), and every
  landmark the cell exists to provide is 8/8 or 6/8 at T200.
- **`techlossforgiveness -1` is also what makes Fighters and Cruise
  Missiles appear at all.** This was not predicted. With the ruleset
  value, two Fighters existed in 320 measured turns and no Cruise
  Missile ever; with -1, Fighters are on the board from T20 (seed 1:
  1,1,1,1,2,1,3,4,4,3,3 — built, lost, rebuilt) and Cruise Missiles
  reach 8 (seed 2). `deep-air` goes 1967 -> 5777 and 10 702, and the
  air arm of the survival wave carries 6769 and 16 338 admissions.
  **These are the first cells in the project's history in which deep's
  air term has a real population** — M4a's table had it at literally
  zero lines everywhere.

So the setting is in the committed config for a measured reason and no
other, and it is an environmental force applied identically to both
paired arms — the standing `migration` already has in `lategame-80`.

### What is NOT met, stated plainly

- **Bombers are never built, and Fighters only in ones and twos.** With
  Advanced Flight known by 8/8 seats from turn 1 across 720 measured
  turns, no Bomber was ever fielded. The AI buys the Helicopter
  (Combined Arms, no fuel) and, heavily, the AEGIS Cruiser (Rocketry,
  30-42 alive) — it is buying air DEFENCE while declining to buy air
  OFFENCE. Fuel-burning types are penalised in stock's own attacker
  pricing (`daimilitary.c:1404-1407` zeroes desire for a fuelled type
  against a target with no defender) and deep inherits the shape.
  **This is the W32 gap itself, not a venue defect** — and it is now
  measurable, which is the whole point of a venue.
- **No eliminations, in any of the four games.** Conquest is real (3-10
  cities change hands per game; 36 and 37 player pairs at war at T200,
  out of 45 orderings among the 8 seats plus barbarians and animals) but no seat was extinguished. The portfolio
  agrees: `contested`@150 was 0/16. The cell makes elimination possible;
  it did not deliver one.

### Verdict

**ACCEPT.** Era reachability, the spy toolkit and the air-threat term
are all met with margin on both seeds, and air units are built and
killed from T20 onward. The elimination clause is not met, and the
strict "Bomber/Fighter classes in quantity" reading is not met either —
both are recorded in the config's own provenance comment. Committed as
`tech80air-200`, ~10 min per game.

## 3. NAVAL cell (task #57)

### Substitute bar

`by_sea` cannot fire (section 0). The counters that do work, and that
answer the same question — *did a wave arrive by water* — are:

- `deep-wave by_transport`: enemy troops **aboard a hull** counted in a
  city's survival wave. Same census, same line, different arm, and this
  arm has no `utype_move_type()` in its path.
- `deep-lift`: player-turns with loaded boats, and with passengers
  waiting for one.
- `deep-invade` go/hold: the overseas-invasion decision.
- **Overseas captures**: cities held by a player who founded nothing on
  that landmass, computed by flood-filling the final save's terrain.

### The candidates

`nav-n3` is `nav-n2`'s world at a higher start; both were run on two
seeds where it mattered.

Three readings:

- **`startpos SINGLE` beats `2or3`.** Given a land neighbour, the AI
  fights the land neighbour: `nav-n1` has 18 city-bearing landmasses and
  7 of them shared, and its seaborne-threat count is the *lowest* of the
  four games. One seat per island is what forces the water crossing to
  be the war.
- **The smaller map beats the larger one** on every naval counter and on
  wall clock, for the obvious reason: on a 32x32 torus the islands are a
  few tiles apart, so a crossing is affordable early rather than late.
- **`techlevel 65` is what turns invasion from an accident into a
  behaviour.** 1 invasion decision per game at 50; 9 and 43 at 65.
  Amphibious Warfare (44 recursive prerequisites, rank 64 of 87) is the
  named reason — 65 is the smallest start that fields Marines, and it
  also brings Transports (12 and 7 alive, against 3) rather than
  Galleons. It is *cheaper* too: 366-412 s against 546 s.

### What is NOT met

- **`by_sea` is 0 in all four games, and section 0 says it always will
  be.** The substitute, `by_transport`, is nonzero in all four and
  reaches 10% of survival evaluations (349/3486) on `nav-n3` seed 2.
- **Overseas CAPTURES stay rare**: 2, 1, 2, 0. The AI forms invasions far
  more often than it lands one — which is exactly the W31/W33 gap this
  cell exists to expose, but it means no claim about landing success can
  be read off a small number of games here.
- **Seed variance in naval activity is large** (9 vs 43 invasion
  decisions on two seeds of the same config). Paired counts only.

### Verdict

**ACCEPT, with the acceptance bar itself corrected.** The stated bar
("`by_sea` nonzero") is unmeetable in principle and its failure is a
code defect, not a venue property. Against the substitute bar the cell
passes on both seeds: seaborne waves are seen, invasions are decided
tens of times per game, Marines exist, and a game costs ~6.5 min.
Committed as `tech65sea-150`.

## 4. What the next session should know

- **Do not re-derive section 0.** `utype_move_type()` is constant
  `UMT_LAND` server-side. Any deep code that asks it a question is
  asking a constant. The repair is `deep_uclass_swims()` /
  `deep_uclass_walks()`, which already exist in `deepdefence.c` and are
  already used for the airborne test; the thirteen listed call sites
  need converting, and `deepcombat.c:590` is the one that costs the
  naval model its whole sea arm.
- **`tech80air-200` is the first venue that can see W32 at all.** It
  should be in any air/SAM/SDI work's acceptance set, and in nothing
  else — it is a deliberately degenerate research regime (80 of 87
  advances at turn 1), so no tech-direction or research-pacing claim may
  cite it.
- **Neither cell delivers eliminations.** If the portfolio needs a war
  that *concludes*, the missing lever is not the horizon — 200 turns did
  not do it at tech 80, and `contested` did not do it at 150 — it is
  probably the player count. That is a separate calibration and it was
  not run here.

## 5. The repair (branch `epic/movetype`, same day)

§0 named thirteen call sites and left them; this section is what
happened to them, and what happened to the AI when they started
working. **Nothing here is merged.** The branch is held for the
overnight A/B named at the end.

### 5.1 No knob

The first attempt put all thirteen behind a `movetype_honest` switch
defaulting to the status quo. That was overruled and the reason is
worth keeping: **there is no defensible "off" value for a comparison
against an enum the server never sets.** A knob offers a choice
between two behaviours; here one side is not a behaviour, it is an
uninitialised field. So the fix is unconditional, exactly as the
credit-integrity fix was earlier the same week, and the discipline
that a knob would have bought — *do not assume, measure* — is bought
instead by holding the branch until the A/B reports.

### 5.2 The mapping, and why it is the strict one

`set_unit_move_type()` derives the field from terrain nativity and
nothing else, and the enum has three values, not four
(`unittype.h:133-137`). **There is no `UMT_AIR`** — a ruleset spells
flight as a class native to both land and ocean, which is why the
airborne test already reads `walks && swims`. So

| enum | honest test |
|---|---|
| `UMT_LAND` | `walks && !swims` |
| `UMT_SEA` | `swims && !walks` |
| `UMT_BOTH` | `walks && swims` — flight |

`deep_utype_is_sea()` and `deep_utype_is_land()` (deepdefence.c, beside
`deep_uclass_swims()`) reproduce what the accessor *would* have
returned, rather than substituting something looser like "it floats".
The difference matters for helicopters: under both predicates a
flying class is neither sea nor land, which is what the old code
meant and what its air-aware callers already assume.

### 5.3 The thirteen sites

Line numbers are pre-fix, as §0 gave them. **No site was benign** —
every one of the thirteen changes behaviour once the test is honest,
which is itself the measure of how thoroughly the constant hid.

**(a) Dead branch — `== UMT_SEA`, never true. The fix makes it fire.**

| site | what never happened | what happens now |
|---|---|---|
| `deepdefence.c:447` | boat-shadow fear | a coastal city feels an enemy hull |
| `deepdefence.c:610` | reachable free land | owning a transport opens overseas sites to the founder pool |
| `deepdefence.c:794` | density-fear cross-continent gate | a rival's army stops being discounted by `menace_overseas_weight` once it floats |
| `deepdefence.c:866` | the same gate in the menace-pressure pass | as above |
| `deepdefence.c:2354` | convoy escort | a warship can be assigned as a transport's bodyguard |
| `deepdefence.c:5344` | overseas berth count | `berths` was always 0, so lift demand was raised however many hulls existed |
| `deepdiplomacy.c:977` | `deep_is_distant()` | a rival with transports is no longer "distant", so the gift lever refuses it |
| `deepcombat.c:612` | the survival wave's sea arm | **the counter this whole finding is named for** |

**(a′) Always-rejecting guard — `!= UMT_SEA`, never false.**

`deepdefence.c:2336` skipped **every** hull, so `deep_manage_convoys()`
was dead in its entirety — not a degraded mechanism, an unexecuted
one.

**(b) Never-rejecting guard — `!= UMT_LAND`, never true, so warships
and aircraft were admitted as land units. The fix rejects them.**

`deepdefence.c:2054` (techwall's wanted defender type could be a ship),
`:2892` (a warship in port could be lent as a garrison blade), `:3518`
(the same, for the relief march), `:5778` (the snap-up sweep's "cheap
fast land attacker" could be a ship).

### 5.4 What it did, measured

Two seeds of `tech65sea-150`, `--arms deep`, jobs 2, identical maps,
one binary built from this branch and one `git archive` build of the
base commit `27cee96378`:

| | base `27cee96` | fix |
|---|---|---|
| `deep-wave` evaluations | 4 676 | **49 215** |
| `by_sea` admissions | **0** | **309 594** |
| ... evaluations with `by_sea` > 0 | 0 | 48 920 (99.4%) |
| `by_land` / `by_transport` | 13 527 / 415 | 4 671 / 984 |
| mean `n_att` | 2.98 | **6.40** |
| mean `surv` | 0.726 | **0.398** |
| `deep-convoy` lines | 0 | 1 |
| wall clock, 2 games | 124 s, 171 s | 145 s, 191 s |
| total score, seed 1 / 2 | 1430 / 1564 | 1627 / 1712 |

The score column is **two seeds and is not a result** (rule 6); it is
here so that a large regression would have been visible, and it was
not. Everything else is a mechanism reading and is not noise: `by_sea`
moved from a proven zero to 98.2% of all qualified admissions on this
venue.

Three things to carry forward, in order of how much trouble they are:

- **The sea arm now dominates the survival model on water venues**
  (98.2% of admissions), and the wave nearly doubled. Mean survival
  fell from 0.73 to 0.40. This is a correctness fix, so the new number
  is the honest one — but **every fear constant in `struct tuning` was
  tuned against the old, warship-blind wave.** That re-tune is real
  debt and belongs to the MIL finale, not here.
- **The sea arm's distance bound is the map.** `sea_max_dist` pegged
  at exactly 18 = `survival_horizon` 3 × a destroyer's move rate 6,
  on a 32×32 torus that is 16 tiles across, and `sea_far` (admissions
  further in *tiles* than the horizon is in *turns*) was 288 699 of
  309 594 — **93%**. The "unbounded distance" worry recorded as
  *deferred, not dead* is now live and large. No bound was invented
  (rule 7); the two counters were added so the population a bound
  would remove is a measured number before anyone picks one.
- **`deep_manage_convoys()` has never run.** It fired once in two
  games after the fix. Whatever it is worth, it has never been worth
  anything yet, and no result that cites it predates this branch.

### 5.5 Scope proof, and what is still owed

A 10-seed paired smoke A/B (`--arms deep,baseline`, jobs 2, same maps,
0 pairing violations, 40 games, no failures) across the two binaries:

- **`baseline` arm: 10/10 games byte-identical in score.** The fix is
  inside `ai/deep` and provably nowhere else.
- `deep` arm: 5/10 games identical, aggregate +0.6%. Directional only.

**The branch is NOT merged and must not be merged on the above.** It
is held for an overnight A/B of this binary against the `27cee96378`
archive build — `tech65sea-150`, `tech50war-200`, and a `contested`
guard — and merges only on that verdict.

Two standing items become downstream consumers of it, and both should
be re-read after the A/B rather than before:

- **The `tech50war-200` NETTHREAT item-1 reading**, which was taken
  against a wave that could not see a warship.
- **The sea-clause verdict** at `deepcombat.c`, which was deferred on
  the grounds that the clause was dead. It is not dead now, and
  §5.4's `sea_far` is the number the verdict should be taken against.
